Vendors

One row per supplier, shared by Inventory and Procurement. Both of those screens used to take the vendor as typed text, so the same supplier spelled two ways was two suppliers to every report, and there was nowhere to keep a GSTIN — the number that decides whether input tax credit can be claimed on a purchase invoice.

Editing a vendor here changes it everywhere at once: correcting a misspelling or a registration number fixes every purchase and order already recorded against it, which is the point of holding it in one place.

No vendors yet. A CPE purchase and a purchase order both name one, so add the supplier here first.

Spend is the cost of this vendor's CPE purchases plus the amount of every purchase order that reached received. A requested or rejected order is not money that went anywhere, so it is left out — counting it would make this column disagree with the general ledger for every order still awaiting a decision, which is exactly when somebody would be reading it.

Retired vendors are listed last rather than hidden: they are where the duplicates to merge and the GSTINs to correct are, and a screen that hid them would hide the work. A vendor is never deleted — its name and GSTIN are what its invoices were issued under, so the row outlives the relationship.

About the GSTIN

Optional, and deliberately so: a vendor gets added the moment somebody needs to record a purchase from them, which is reliably before anyone has dug the registration number out of an invoice. Demanding it up front would mean either a blocked purchase or an invented number — and an invented one is worse than none, because it would look authoritative in the master and be claimed against a registration that does not exist.

What is entered is checked twice: that it is fifteen characters in the shape the registry issues, and that its last character matches the checksum of the other fourteen. The second catch is the useful one — a transposed pair of digits is still a perfectly well-shaped GSTIN, and the only moment that mistake is cheap to fix is while the invoice is still in your hand.

Two live vendors cannot share a GSTIN, because a GSTIN is one legal entity. If the system refuses one on those grounds, the other row is the same company under a different spelling, and merging them is the work this screen exists for.

Phone numbers here are not validated

Unlike a subscriber's or a franchise partner's, which have to be E.164 because the notification pipeline dials and texts them. Nothing in this system ever contacts a vendor, so the only reader is a person with the phone in their hand — and a supplier's number is routinely a landline with an extension, which E.164 cannot express. Constraining it would refuse correct data to serve a purpose this field does not have.
