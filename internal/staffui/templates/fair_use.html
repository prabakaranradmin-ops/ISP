Fair-use policies

A fair-use policy is the set of steps a plan takes once a subscriber is over quota: cross the first threshold and their speed drops, cross a harder one and it drops further. Plans share policies, so an operator runs a handful of shapes across many tariffs.

Editing a policy re-shapes every plan on it — subscribers move tier on the scanner's next pass, within a minute, not immediately. The first threshold is also the figure the 80% warning is measured against, because that is where a customer's speed actually changes.

Up to {{.MaxTiers}} steps. Thresholds are cumulative usage in the cycle, so a plan that throttles at 500GB and harder at 1TB has steps of 500 and 1000, not 500 and 500.

A throttle is a flat ceiling written "down/up". It is deliberately not a bandwidth profile: giving a throttled subscriber a burst would hand back the speed the throttle exists to remove.

Which policy a plan uses

A plan with no policy keeps using its own single threshold and throttle. That is a supported setup, not something left half-done.
